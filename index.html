Traveling Through Hexes

When traveling from one city to another, or distances that are measured on the world map, we will be using special rules. When traveling over land, the party is assumed to move around 24 mi/day (one hex) for an 8-hour day of walking. However, you may choose to move faster to get to your destination quicker, or slower to attract less attention from others who may also be on the road. (Note: For slow and fast walking paces, you will only see the benefit over more than one day of using the pace. I.e., no slow-fast-slow-etc.) These speeds are listed in the table below.

    • Rules for Traveling

    We will run traveling days in stages. The first stage occurs on all days (steps 1-2). The second stage (steps 3-4) only occurs if there are no towns or cities that the party can pass through on their journey:

    1. Each day, the party will choose 1 character to act as the party’s guide for the day and 2 others to be on watch duty for the night (if applicable, see 3-4).
    2. The guide directs where the party goes and makes a Traversal Check against the hex’s terrain DC, and the party moves in the chosen direction.
    3. Traversal Checks are a Skill Check from among the following: Acrobatics, Athletics, History, Nature, or Survival.
    4. Failing a Traversal Check results in the party getting overworked, lost, or just drained. Each party member gains a level of exhaustion.

    The following steps then only occur if there is no large enough settlement in the current hex:

    1. The party makes a group (averaged) Stealth check to see if anything encounters them on their travels  (DC hidden).
    2. At night, two members chosen for watch duty make a group (averaged) Perception or Investigation check to pass the night (DC hidden).

    Unfortunately, doing a job for more than 2 days (3 for Chia and Eldith) in a row is very tiring. You gain a level of exhaustion for each day in a row you do a job after the second day.
